The private markets opportunity set has grown considerably over the past decade. More capital, more vehicles, more intermediaries. The natural consequence is compression — of returns, of differentiation, and eventually of conviction.

In this environment, quality becomes the last remaining edge.

## What quality means here

Quality in private markets is not simply a matter of sector or size. It is a function of durability: the degree to which a business can sustain its economics through a full cycle without requiring extraordinary conditions to justify the entry price.

The markers are familiar. Pricing power. Low capital intensity relative to returns. Management with genuine alignment. A competitive position that is difficult to replicate quietly, without fanfare.

What matters most is not the category but the coherence — whether the business model, the competitive position, and the financial structure all point in the same direction over time.

## The discipline of selectivity

Selectivity is harder than it sounds. The instinct under deployment pressure is to expand the definition of quality rather than to miss an opportunity. This is how mediocre businesses acquire premium labels.

The correct response is the opposite: tighten the definition as the opportunity set grows noisier. Accept that a smaller pipeline of genuine conviction is worth more than a larger pipeline of approximate fit.

The best private market investors have always known this. The current environment merely makes the lesson more expensive to ignore.
